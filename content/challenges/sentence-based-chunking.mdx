# Sentence-Based Chunking: Respecting Language

## The Linguistic Insight

Characters and words are arbitrary. **Sentences** are the smallest complete thoughts in language.

```
Bad chunk:  "Machine learning is"     ← incomplete thought
Good chunk: "Machine learning is a subset of AI."  ← complete thought
```

---

## Why Sentences Matter for Embeddings

Embeddings capture **meaning**. A sentence like "The cat sat on the mat" has clear meaning.

But "The cat sat on the" is meaningless - the embedding will be noisy and unreliable.

---

## The Algorithm

```python
import re

def sentence_chunk(text: str, max_sentences: int = 3) -> List[str]:
    # Split on sentence boundaries
    sentences = re.split(r'(?<=[.!?])\s+', text)

    # Group into chunks of N sentences
    chunks = []
    for i in range(0, len(sentences), max_sentences):
        chunk = ' '.join(sentences[i:i + max_sentences])
        chunks.append(chunk)
    return chunks
```

---

## ⚖️ Tradeoffs

### Pros

- ✅ Complete thoughts preserved
- ✅ Better embedding quality
- ✅ More natural for human review

### Cons

- ❌ Highly variable chunk sizes
- ❌ One sentence might be 10 words or 100 words
- ❌ Complex sentences with semicolons are tricky

---

## Handling Edge Cases

Real-world sentence detection is harder than you think:

```
"Dr. Smith works at Inc. Corp. He is an expert."
```

A naive regex would split at "Dr." and "Inc." incorrectly.

**Solutions**:

- Use spaCy's `sentencizer`
- Use NLTK's `sent_tokenize`
- Custom rules for abbreviations

| Content Type    | Avg Sentence Length |
| --------------- | ------------------- |
| Tweets          | 15-20 words         |
| News articles   | 20-25 words         |
| Academic papers | 30-40 words         |
| Legal documents | 50+ words           |

You may need to combine with size limits to handle outliers.

Sentence chunking teaches us:

> "Linguistic boundaries > Arbitrary character counts"

Language has natural structure. Respect it.
