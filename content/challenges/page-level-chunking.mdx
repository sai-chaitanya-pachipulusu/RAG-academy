  # Page-Level Chunking


  Many documents have natural page boundaries that authors intentionally use to
  organize content. Research shows page-level chunking often performs
  surprisingly well - sometimes beating complex methods!


  ## The Problem


  Traditional chunking ignores page boundaries, potentially splitting content
  that was designed to stay together:

  - **Slides**: Each slide is a complete unit of thought

  - **PDFs**: Headers/footers reset per page, visual layouts are page-bound

  - **Scanned docs**: Page = natural retrieval and citation unit


  ## Your Task


  Implement `page_level_chunk()` that:

  1. Treats each page as a potential chunk

  2. Merges very short pages with adjacent ones

  3. Tracks original page numbers for citation

  4. Returns structured output with page metadata


  ## Why This Matters


  - **Citation accuracy**: "See page 42" is much clearer than "see chunk 7 of
  document 3"

  - **Layout preservation**: Keeps visually-related content together

  - **Simplicity**: Often beats complex semantic chunking on well-structured
  PDFs


  ## Real-World Usage


  - **LlamaParse**: Uses page-level as initial parsing strategy

  - **Unstructured.io**: Page boundaries as primary document structure

  - **Legal/Financial docs**: Page citations are often legally required
difficulty: medium

Page-level chunking treats document pages as natural chunk boundaries. This fundamental technique recognizes that document authors intentionally organize content by pages - a fact that sophisticated chunking methods often overlook.
